## Overview

Citation fabrication — the confident generation of false or misattributed bibliographic references by large language models (LLMs) and agentic search tools — has emerged as a critical failure mode with measurable consequences for the scientific record. While unaugmented chat models fabricate references at rates exceeding 50%, even retrieval-augmented systems and deep-research agents produce citations with non-existent URLs or incorrect metadata at 3–18%. This repository synthesizes benchmarking evidence, detection methods, and mitigation strategies published between 2023 and 2026 across biomedicine, law, computer science, and general-purpose search.

### Key Questions

1. **Citation Authenticity**: Does the cited publication actually exist and is it correctly identified?
2. **Claim-Citation Support**: If the publication is genuine, does it actually support the claim for which it is cited?

### Central Finding

Citation fabrication is not solely a model-capability problem — it depends critically on:
- Whether the system has access to retrieval and external sources
- The domain and coverage of the training/retrieval corpus
- Output modality (text vs. hyperlinks)
- The **volume effect**: even low per-citation hallucination rates compound into measurable absolute fabrication when systems generate many citations per output
